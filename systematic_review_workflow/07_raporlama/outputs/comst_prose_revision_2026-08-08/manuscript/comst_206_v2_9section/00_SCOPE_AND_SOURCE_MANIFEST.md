## Scientific purpose

The paper is an O-ISAC survey, not a report about conducting PRISMA. PRISMA and
PRISMA-S make the evidence assembly traceable; the scientific center is the
cross-platform explanation of optical architectures, integration mechanisms,
measurement meaning, tradeoffs, validation maturity, applications, and the
conditions required for cumulative research.

The local corpus of 76 eligible IEEE Communications Surveys & Tutorials
papers supplies descriptive architecture and prose guardrails. It does not
authorize copying source language or filling scientific gaps by imitation.

## Evidence and language boundaries

- O-ISAC eligibility follows the locked broad operational definition. A
  stricter shared-resource rule is not introduced after the fact.
- A numerical comparison across studies requires visible metric meaning,
  measurement plane, task, operating conditions, baseline where relevant, and
  validation setting.
- Range resolution, estimator error, precision, and analytical bounds remain
  distinct. Electrical SNR, OSNR, optical power, BER, gross rate, and net rate
  are not interchangeable.
- TQAF is a review-specific technical appraisal, not conventional risk of bias
  or GRADE.
- The OSF record is retrospective. Its 221-study state is a superseded
  predecessor and is not attrition to 206.
- One investigator supervised the workflow and made the final review
  decisions. Routine independent duplicate human review was not performed.
- No meta-analysis, formal missing-results/publication-bias assessment, or
  formal sensitivity analysis was performed.
- No first, only, most comprehensive, universal frontier, or cross-platform
  ranking claim is permitted.

## Nine-section reader journey

1. Introduction, including prior-survey positioning and Table I.
2. O-ISAC foundations and the comparison framework.
3. Review method and evidence base.
4. Optical platforms and integration architectures.
5. Performance metrics and joint-design tradeoffs.
6. Validation evidence, reconstructability, and benchmark readiness.
7. Enabling technologies, applications, and 6G implications.
8. Discussion, research roadmap, and limitations.
9. Conclusion.

This order follows the dominant COMST reader journey: motivate the problem,
establish the comparison language, disclose how evidence was assembled,
explain the technical families, evaluate performance and maturity, and then
derive a bounded research agenda.
