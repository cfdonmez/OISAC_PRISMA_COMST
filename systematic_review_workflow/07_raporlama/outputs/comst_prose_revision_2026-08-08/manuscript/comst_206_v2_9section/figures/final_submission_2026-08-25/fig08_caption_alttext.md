# Figure 8 text

## Lead in

Figure 8 follows the O-ISAC evidence chain from optical generation and
transport through waveform formation, spatial control, inference, application
requirements, and 6G network positioning.

## Caption

Cross layer organization of the mechanisms that create and shape O-ISAC
observables and connect them to service requirements. Technology and
application counts are multilabel corpus coverage, so a study may contribute
to several displayed categories. The 6G evidence gate is exclusive and assigns
each of the 206 studies to one relevance class. Selected application domains
illustrate the requirement flow, while Table VII provides the complete
application inventory.

## After prose

The chain shows that network interpretation depends on continuity across the
physical observable, the joint design choice, the application condition, and
the reported network role. The displayed categories provide navigation through
that relationship, while the corresponding sections retain the platform
conditions and study evidence.

## Alt text

Six connected layers run from left to right. Optical generation and transport
contains photonic terahertz generation with 68 studies, coherent optical
techniques with 64, fiber distributed acoustic sensing with 22, and photonic
integration with 20. Waveform and observables contains frequency modulated
continuous wave processing with 66 and orthogonal frequency division
multiplexing with 56. Spatial control contains beamforming with 13, optical
phased arrays with 8, and multiple input multiple output processing with 7.
Inference and system models contains machine learning or artificial
intelligence with 20 and digital twins with 2. Four selected application
domains illustrate requirement families through vehicular with 41,
environmental monitoring with 55, indoor positioning with 13, and aerospace
with 20. The final 6G gate assigns 138 studies to direct relevance, 64 to
inferential relevance, 1 to weak relevance, and 3 to the not applicable class.
Arrows show progression from physical mechanisms to application requirements
and network evidence.
