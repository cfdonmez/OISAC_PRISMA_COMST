**Components:**
- **Tx bits** -> **Modulation** (Pink block) -> **Hermitian Symmetry** (Orange block) -> **IFFT & Adding CP** (Orange block) -> **DAC** (Orange block) -> **DC bias & Clipping** (Orange block) -> **Laser diode** (Orange block).
- **FSO-ISAC Transceiver** (Purple box): Includes **Photodiode**, **ADC**, **MLE** (Green block) -> **Target Distance**. Use of **x(t)** and **x+(t)** signals.
- **Wireless Channel:** **Target/Reflector** shown with zig-zag lines.
- **Communication Receiver** (Beige box): **Photodiode**, **ADC**, **FFT** -> **Equalization** (Pink block) -> **Demodulation** (Pink block) -> **Rx bits**.
- **Signals:** X(k,m), x(n), x(t), x+(t) [Transmitter]; y_s(t), y_s(n) [Sensing Rx]; y_c(t), y_c(n), Y_c(k,m) [Comm Rx].

**Flow:**
1.  **Transmission:** Tx bits are modulated, processed with Hermitian Symmetry and IFFT/CP to create an OFDM signal. This digital signal x(n) is converted to analog x(t) by a DAC, then biased and clipped (DC bias & Clipping) to produce the positive signal x+(t) which drives the Laser diode.
2.  **Sensing Path:** The optical signal reflects off a target. The echo is captured by the FSO-ISAC transceiver's Photodiode, converted by an ADC to y_s(n), and processed by an MLE block to estimate Target Distance.
3.  **Communication Path:** Part of the signal reaches the Communication receiver, is detected by a Photodiode, converted by an ADC to y_c(n), processed by FFT, Equalized, and Demodulated to recover Rx bits.

**Axes Extraction:**
- **X-axis:** Iteration [Range: 1 to 60]
- **Y-axis:** Normalized residual ε [Range: 10^-3 to 10^0] (Log scale)

**Data Points:**
- **At Iteration 1:** All curves start at residual = 1 (10^0).
- **c/2ζ = 4.00 cm (Red diamonds):** Converges fastest. Reaches 10^-3 around Iteration 16.
- **c/2ζ = 3.75 cm (Blue stars):** Reaches 10^-3 around Iteration 21.
- **c/2ζ = 3.50 cm (Green triangles):** Reaches 10^-3 around Iteration 29.
- **c/2ζ = 3.25 cm (Purple triangles):** Reaches 10^-3 around Iteration 46.
- **c/2ζ = 3.00 cm (Orange circles):** Converges slowest. At Iteration 60, residual is still approx 10^-3.

**Legend/Trends:**
- **Variables:** Precision parameter (c/2ζ) ranging from 3.00 cm to 4.00 cm.
- **Trend:** stricter sensing precision requirements (smaller values like 3.00 cm) lead to slower convergence of the algorithm (more iterations needed to reach the same residual). Relaxed precision (4.00 cm) converges much faster.

---
**Axes Extraction:**
- **X-axis:** Subcarrier index k [Range: 0 to 127]
- **Y-axis:** Normalized power allocation P[k] [Range: 0 to 0.02]

**Data Points:**
- **Optimal comm. (Red horizontal line):** Constant/uniform power allocation across all subcarriers at approx 0.004.
- **Optimal sensing (Blue line):** Zero power for k < ~102. Spikes to max power (saturated at 0.02) for k > ~102.
- **Trade-off curves (c/2ζ values):**
    - **c/2ζ = 4.00 cm (Light Blue):** Starts near 0.003 at k=0, rises gradually to ~0.009 at k=127. Closest to "Optimal comm" uniform profile.
    - **c/2ζ = 3.00 cm (Orange):** Starts near 0.001 at k=0, rises sharply for high k, reaching 0.02 (saturation) around k=115. approaches "Optimal sensing" behavior.
- **Intersection:** All "Trade-off" curves intersect near subcarrier index k=100-102.

**Legend/Trends:**
- **Variables:** Optimal Sensing vs Optimal Comm vs Trade-off formulations with varying precision (c/2ζ).
- **Trend:** "Optimal comm" prefers uniform power allocation. "Optimal sensing" prefers concentrating power in high-frequency subcarriers (high k). The "Trade-off" curves transition from uniform-like to high-frequency-focused as the sensing precision requirement becomes stricter (smaller c/2ζ).

**Axes Extraction:**
- **X-axis:** Precision c/2ζ (cm) [Range: 2.7 to 5]
- **Y-axis:** Spectral efficiency C (bps/Hz) [Range: 0 to 4]

**Data Points:**
- **Cut-off:** For all noise levels, C drops to 0 below approx precision 2.7 cm (infeasibility region).
- **N_c = -70 dBm/Hz (Blue circle - Best Channel):** Rises sharply from 0 to ~3.5 bps/Hz at precision 3.25 cm. Saturates/plateaus around 3.95 bps/Hz for precision > 4.5 cm.
- **N_c = -57 dBm/Hz (Green triangle - Worst Channel):** Rises slower. Reaches ~1.8 bps/Hz at precision 3.25 cm. Plateaus around 2.2 bps/Hz.
- **Saturation:** All curves saturate as precision requirement is relaxed (becomes larger), indicating that beyond ~4 cm, further relaxing sensing constraints yields minimal gain in spectral efficiency.

**Legend/Trends:**
- **Variables:** Noise power spectral density N_c (-70 to -57 dBm/Hz).
- **Trend:** Lower noise (N_c = -70 dBm/Hz) yields significantly higher spectral efficiency. There is a sharp trade-off region between 2.7 cm and 3.5 cm precision where small relaxations in sensing precision yield massive gains in communication capacity (Spectral Efficiency).
