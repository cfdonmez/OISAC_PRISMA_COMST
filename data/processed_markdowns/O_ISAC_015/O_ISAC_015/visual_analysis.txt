### Image 1: _page_1_Figure_10.jpeg  
- **Type**: Diagram  
- **Description**: A simplified setup diagram illustrating an indoor corridor environment for Visible Light Positioning (VLP) experiments. Key elements include a Medium Access Point (MAP), a communication user (green cylinder), a non-user LOS (Line-of-Sight) blocker (red cylinder), a sensing user (yellow cylinder), slide tracks, and devices mounted on walls.  
- **KEY DATA**:  
  - 20 Access Points (APs) installed ceiling-mounted.  
  - Blocks (non-user LOS blockers) are positioned between communication/sensing users and APs.  
  - Communication and sensing users are positioned at ends of the corridor.  
  - Slide tracks enable motion-based VLP (e.g., movement at 1.4 m/s).

### Image 2: _page_3_Figure_10.jpeg  
- **Type**: Line Chart  
- **Description**: Compares aggregate sum rate and mutual information (MI) of a VLP system with varying numbers of non-user LOS blockers (0–10 blockers).  
- **KEY DATA**:  
  - **Proposed method**:  
    - 20 APs, P=0.5 W: ~350 Gbps (stable with >8 blockers).  
    - 15 APs, P=0.5 W: ~275 Gbps.  
    - 10 APs, P=0.5 W: ~150 Gbps.  
  - **Benchmark**:  
    - High power (P=20 W): ~275 Gbps (degraded with blockers).  
    - Lower power (P=5 W): ~100 Gbps.  
  - **Trend**: Proposed method with 20 APs minimizes blocker impact, while benchmarks show significant degradation as blockers increase.

### Image 4: _page_4_Figure_2.jpeg  
- **Type**: Line Chart  
- **Description**: Aggregated sum rate over time (0–4 seconds) for proposed and benchmark VLP users in a dynamic corridor setup.  
- **KEY DATA**:  
  - **Proposed method**:  
    - Communication user 2 (brown solid): Starts at ~10,000 Gbps, drops to ~1,000 Gbps.  
    - Sensing user 1 (green dotted): Starts at ~200 Gbps, drops to ~50 Gbps.  
  - **Benchmark**:  
    - Communication user 2 (blue dashed): Starts at ~350 Gbps, drops to ~150 Gbps.  
    - Sensing user 2 (magenta dashed): Starts at ~150 Gbps, drops to ~100 Gbps.  
  - **Trend**: Proposed methods retain higher rates over time compared to benchmarks during user movement.

### Image 5: _page_4_Figure_4.jpeg  
- **Type**: Line Chart  
- **Description**: Relationship between priority of communication users and average sum rate/MI. Priority ranges from 0 (lowest) to 1 (highest).  
- **KEY DATA**:  
  - **6 users**:  
    - Sum rate: Sharp increase from ~50 Gbps to ~150 Gbps as priority increases (0.2–0.8).  
    - MI: Declines from ~200 Gbps to ~50 Gbps for 10 users.  
  - **10 users**:  
    - Sum rate: Accelerated rise after priority >0.7 (e.g., ~100 Gbps at priority 0.9).  
    - MI: Steeper drop compared to 6 users.  
  - **Trend**: Higher user priority improves rate but reduces MI, highlighting trade-offs in resource allocation.
