# O_ISAC_154 Visual Analysis

## 1. Event Overview (Fig. 1)
- **Subject**: Calbuco volcano eruption, Chile (April 2015).
- **Location**: -41.33° S, -72.61° W. Summit altitude: 2003 m.
- **Intensity**: Sub-Plinian eruption with ash plume reaching >10 km.

## 2. Imaging Sensors and Complementarity
- **Sensors**: 
    - **VIIRS (TIR)**: 750 m nadir footprint. Bands: 0.746–12.013 µm.
    - **ATMS (MW)**: 15.8 km to 74.8 km resolution. Bands: 23.8–183.31 GHz.
- **Key Observation (Fig. 2 & 3)**: Thermal Infrared (TIR) brightness temperatures (BT) saturate near the vent due to high optical extinction. Brightness Temperature Difference (BTD) is almost zero at the source but effective for distal ash tracking.
- **Microwave Role (Fig. 4 & 5)**: MW signatures (89–183 GHz) are detectable for the near-source plume (05:09 UTC) as they do not saturate.

## 3. Tephra Detection and Retrieval (Fig. 6, 7 & 8)
- **Methodology**: 
    - **Detection**: Microwave Spectra Difference in Window (MSDW) = BT(165 GHz) - BT(88.2 GHz).
    - **Resampling**: MW images resampled to match TIR grids (Fig. 6).
- **Physical Estimates**:
    - **Mass Loading (Fig. 7)**: Values up to ~9 kg/m² estimated from 183.3 GHz BT (linear predictor: $L_t = 112.23 - 0.43 \cdot BT$).
    - **Plume Top Height ($H_t$)**: Maximum estimated height ~18.7 km ASL (polynomial estimator).
    - **Mass Flow Rate (MFR, Fig. 8)**: Peaks at ~1.7 x 10⁷ kg/s, characteristic of intense eruptions.

## 4. Limitations
- **Resolution**: MW observations (15 km) have poor spatial resolution compared to TIR (1 km).
- **Sensitivity**: MW is less sensitive to distal, fine ash clouds compared to TIR.
