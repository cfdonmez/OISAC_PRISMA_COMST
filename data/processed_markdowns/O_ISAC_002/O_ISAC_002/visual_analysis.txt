This image consists of three rows of conceptual diagrams, each illustrating different signal processing or multiplexing techniques.

*   **Top Row:** Diagram. This row illustrates three advanced multiplexing techniques: Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), Orthogonal Time Frequency Space (OTFS), and Orthogonal Chirp Division Multiplexing (OCDM). OFDM is depicted with overlapping frequency-domain subcarriers derived from an inverse Fourier transform. OTFS shows a grid in time-frequency space, transformed by a Symplectic Finite Fourier Transform. OCDM displays multiple chirped signals in the time domain, resulting from an inverse Fresnel transform. These represent different signal characteristics and transformations in various communication paradigms.
*   **Middle Row:** Diagram. This row explains the principle of Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum (DSSS). A baseband "Sequence" (a time-domain digital signal) is combined with a "Spread Spectrum" signal (represented by a wideband frequency spectrum with a gain). The result, "Direct Sequence Spread Spectrum," shows the original sequence's pulses modulated by a higher-rate spreading code, leading to a wider signal bandwidth in the time domain.
*   **Bottom Row:** Diagram. This row demonstrates the creation of an LFM-PSK (Linear Frequency Modulation - Phase Shift Keying) waveform. "Linear Frequency Modulation" (LFM) shows a chirp signal (frequency changes linearly with time). This is combined with a "Single-carrier Modulation" (a carrier wave). The resulting "LFM-PSK Waveform" is illustrated in a mixed domain plot, indicating the integration of both frequency (chirp) and amplitude/phase modulation, often used in radar or robust communication.
