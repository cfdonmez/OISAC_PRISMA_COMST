Components:
- Tx Antenna: Transmitter for the ISAC signal.
- Rx Antenna: Receiver for target reflections and communication signals.
- PD (Photodetector): Interfaces the optical signal to the RF domain for transmission.
- LNA (Low Noise Amplifier): Amplifies the weak received RF signal.
- Transmission and Reception Layer: Middleware handling signal routing.
- Baseband SIC: Digital self-interference cancellation block.
- Signal Modulators and Demodulators: Baseband processing units for comms.

Flow:
1. Signal Modulator produces the baseband signal.
2. The signal is routed through the Transmission layer to the PD and Tx antenna.
3. Crucially, a "Self-Interference Signal" path represents leakage from Tx to Rx antennas (RF Analog Signal SIC).
4. The Rx antenna captures the target reflection + interference.
5. The LNA amplifies the composite signal.
6. The Baseband SIC block uses a reference from the Tx path (blue arrow) to cancel the interference in the Rx path (pink arrow) before the signal reaches the Demodulator.

---
Components:
- LD1, LD2, LD3: Laser Diodes providing optical carriers (193.1 THz and LO).
- DAC: Generates the digital LFM-BPSK waveform.
- MZM / DPMZM: Mach-Zehnder Modulators for signal/LO and SIC modulation.
- OC1, OC2, OC3: Optical Couplers for signal splitting/combining.
- Horn Antennas: mmW interfaces for transmission and reception.
- EA / ED: Electrical Amplifier and Delay for reference signal balancing.
- PD1: Generates the 100 GHz mmW signal.
- PD2: Coherent receiver photodetector.
- Mixer/LO1/LPF: RF down-conversion chain.
- ADC: Captures the final processed signal for DSP.

Flow:
1. LD1 is modulated by the ISAC signal (from DAC) using an MZM.
2. This is combined with LD2 and sent to PD1 to generate a 100 GHz mmW carrier for the Tx antenna.
3. A copy of the Tx signal (RF) is taken, adjusted via ED (delay) and EA (amplitude).
4. This reference signal is used to modulate sub-MZM2 of a DPMZM (biased at MITP).
5. The received signal (from Rx antenna/LNA1) modulates sub-MZM1.
6. Spectral Diagrams (A, B, C) illustrate the cancellation:
   - A: Self-interference spectra at point A.
   - B: Reference signal spectra at point B (anti-phase).
   - C: Resultant spectra at point C showing SI suppression and the target signal.
7. The output is processed through a heterodyne receiver (PD2/Mixer/ADC).

- Axes Extraction: X-axis: Time (ns), 0 to 100. Y-axis: Normalized Amplitude, -1 to 1.
- Legend: DAC generated (blue) vs Post ADC (red).
- Trend: The recovered BPSK signal (red) tracks the reference (blue) almost perfectly, indicating successful demodulation after interference removal.

Chart (c) - Radar Reception (Ranging)
- Axes Extraction: X-axis: Time (ns), -50 to 50. Y-axis: Normalized Amplitude, 0 to 0.9.
- Data Points: A sharp ranging peak is clearly visible at **30 ns**, matching the simulated target delay distance.

Chart (d) - Radar Reception (Zoomed Correlation)
- Axes Extraction: X-axis: Time (ns), -0.6 to 0.6. Y-axis: Normalized Amplitude, 0 to 1.
- Data Points: The **PSLR (Peak-to-Side-Lobe Ratio) is calculated as 15.35 dB**, demonstrating high sensing resolution and low floor noise.
