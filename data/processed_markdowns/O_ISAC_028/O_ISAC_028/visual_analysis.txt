## Figure 2: Pairwise Coding (PWC) Logic
**File:** [_page_1_Figure_15.jpeg](file:///g:/Drive'ım/AKU_WorkSpace/survey_fdgit/OISAC_PRISMA_COMST/data/processed_markdowns/O_ISAC_028/O_ISAC_028/_page_1_Figure_15.jpeg)
- **(a) Encoding:** 16-QAM symbols ($a_n, b_n$) undergo 45° angle rotation, followed by I/Q interleaving. This maps the information across "Good" and "Bad" subcarriers to spread the noise penalty.
- **(b) Decoding:** Rescaling by measured SNR, de-interleaving, and Maximum Likelihood Detection (MLD).

## Figure 5 & 6: Sensitivity Gains
**File:** [_page_3_Figure_7.jpeg](file:///g:/Drive'ım/AKU_WorkSpace/survey_fdgit/OISAC_PRISMA_COMST/data/processed_markdowns/O_ISAC_028/O_ISAC_028/_page_3_Figure_7.jpeg) & [_page_3_Figure_9.jpeg](file:///g:/Drive'ım/AKU_WorkSpace/survey_fdgit/OISAC_PRISMA_COMST/data/processed_markdowns/O_ISAC_028/O_ISAC_028/_page_3_Figure_9.jpeg)
- **Optimal Point:** CSPR = **5 dB** for all OSNR cases (28, 30, 32 dB).
- **OSNR Gain:** 
  - At the **7% HD-FEC** limit, PWC provides a **1.5 dB OSNR gain** over the traditional scheme.
  - This gain allows for lower receiver sensitivity requirements in 256 Gb/s short-reach links.
