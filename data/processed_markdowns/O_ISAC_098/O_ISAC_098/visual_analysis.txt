Type: Application Scenario Schematic
---
**Components:**
* **Transmitter:** Source of the optical signal.
* **OIRS (Optical Intelligent Reflecting Surface):** Reflects the signal to the target.
* **Beams:** Narrow beam (red) and Wide beam (yellow cone).
* **User:** Automobile with an "Optical communication receiver" and a "PD array" for sensing.
* **Feedback:** "Uplink signal (OIRS control)" represented by a dashed blue line from User to OIRS.
**Flow:** Transmitter sends signal to OIRS -> OIRS directs a wide beam for sensing and a narrow beam for communication to the moving User -> User feedback (relative position) helps OIRS adjust the narrow beam direction.

Type: Operational Logic Flowchart
---
**Phases:**
* **Stage 1: Wide beam sensing stage:** Estimate relative position -> Generate OIRS control signals -> Uplink transmission to OIRS.
* **Stage 2: Beam alignment stage:** Process control signals -> Adjust narrow beam direction.
**System Loop:** Base Station -> OIRS -> User -> (Feedback through Stage 1/2) -> OIRS.

Image: _page_11_Figure_2.jpeg
Type: Performance Chart (BER vs Pt) - Weak Turbulence
---
**Axes:** X: $P_t$ (dBm) (10 to 35), Y: BER ($10^0$ to $10^{-7}$).
**Parameters:** $\sigma_R^2 = 0.05$.
**Data:**
* At $P_t = 25$ dBm, BER for $\theta_o = 2 \times 10^{-3}$ and $S_w/S = 0.3$ is approx $10^{-4}$.
* **Trends:** Increasing beam offset ($\theta_o$) or wide-to-narrow beam area ratio ($S_w/S$) degrades BER.
* **Comparison:** The proposed scheme significantly outperforms the "Traditional FSO link without beam alignment" (dashed cyan) which maintains BER > $10^{-3}$ even at 30 dBm.

---
**Axes:** X: $P_t$ (dBm) (10 to 35), Y: Outage Probability ($10^0$ to $10^{-6}$).
