Image: _page_5_Figure_2.jpeg
Type: System Diagram / Geometric Schematic
---
**Components:**
- **Epicenter:** Origin of seismic waves (Blue dot).
- **P0, P1:** Sensors/Reference points on the horizontal axis (Submarine cable).
- **P2:** Obstacle or secondary point.
- **Parameters:** $D_0, D_1$ (Distances to sensors), $V_P, V_S$ (Wave velocities), $L_f$ (Distance between sensors), $\theta$ (Arrival angle).

**Flow:**
The diagram defines the triangulation geometry for epicenter localization. Seismic waves (P and S waves) arrive at two points ($P_0, P_1$) on a fiber cable. By measuring the time-of-arrival differences ($\Delta t$) at these points separated by $L_f$, the angle of arrival $\theta$ and the distance to the epicenter can be calculated based on the velocity relationship $V_{fs} \approx V_S / \cos\theta$.
---

Image: _page_5_Figure_7.jpeg
Type: Flowchart / Algorithm
---
**Steps:**
1. Get $V_S$ based on Seismological Bureau reports.
2. Get $V_{fs}$ along the fiber: $V_{fs} = L_f / (\Delta t_{S0} - \Delta t_{S1})$, and get $\cos\theta = V_S / V_{fs}$.
3. Get epicenter distance ratio $D_0/D_1$ using P-S wave time differences.
4. Final Calculation: Get $D_0$ and $D_1$ based on $L_f, \cos\theta, D_0/D_1$ using the provided quadratic formula.

**Flow:**
Outlines the step-by-step mathematical procedure to calculate the exact distance to an earthquake's epicenter using DAS cable sensing data and local seismic velocity reports.
