Type: System Diagram / Schematic (Decoupling Principle)
---
Components:
- Welding spots (45° and 90°): Precise polarization-maintaining fiber alignment points.
- Measurement Arm (M) and Reference Arm (R).
- Polarization States: Fast axis (f) and Slow axis (s).
- Decoupled Components: Mf, Ms, Rf, Rs.
- Phase Outputs: φMfRs and φMsRf (Dual-interferometer outputs).
- Coefficient Matrices: Strain coefficients (ε) and Temperature coefficients (T).

Flow:
1. Input light is split into two orthogonal paths (90° welding spot).
2. Each path is rotated (45° welding spots) to launch equally into the Measurement and Reference arms.
3. The arms are recombined such that the fast-axis measurement component (Mf) interferes with the slow-axis reference (Rs), and vice versa for (Ms, Rf) via a PMFC.
4. Two distinct phase fluctuations are extracted at 0° and 90° outputs.
5. These phases are processed through the shown data preprocessing matrix to extract Strain (ε) and Temperature (ΔT) independently.
---

---
Components:
- Light Source: Stable laser for interferometer.
- FC/APC: Fiber connector with physical contact.
- Y-Waveguide: Splits the light into two paths.
- MA (Measurement Arm): Coiled fiber on the sensor disk.
- RA (Reference Arm): Coiled fiber on the sensor disk.
- PMFC (Polarization-Maintaining Fiber Coupler): Recombines light.
- 0° LP and 90° LP: Linear Polarizers for state selection.
- PD (Photodiodes): Convert optical signals to electrical phase data.

Flow:
- Light passes from the source through the Y-waveguide to the MA and RA.
- Interference occurs at the PMFC and is detected by dual PDs after polarization filtering.

Image: _page_2_Picture_6.jpeg
Type: System Diagram / Schematic (Mechanical Sensor Design)
---
Components:
- Metal shell: Protective outer casing of the FOVSU.
- Elastic disk: Flexible platform for strain transfer.
- Mass block: Inertial mass converting acceleration into force.
- Sensitive Fiber Coil (SFC): 260m of fiber wound on the disk.

Flow:
1. External vibration/acceleration causes the Mass block to move.
2. The inertia of the mass block deforms the Elastic disk.
3. This deformation induces radial strain in the SFC.
4. The strain manifests as a phase shift in the optical signal traveling through the SFC, enabling sensing.
---

Image: _page_2_Picture_13.jpeg
Type: System Diagram / Schematic (Thermal Model)
---
Components:
- T1, T2: Ambient temperatures at different longitudinal segments.
- T3: External boundary temperature.
- T4: Internal fiber core temperature gradient.
- Heat conduction arrows (red/blue): Representing radial and longitudinal heat flow.

Flow:
1. Ambient temperature changes initiate heat conduction through the metal shell and SFC.
2. Non-uniform thermal gradients (T1 to T4) develop across the fiber loops.
3. These gradients create nonlinear phase shifts that standard linear compensation methods fail to handle, justifying the need for the piecewise uniform compensation strategy.
---

Type: Flowchart (Algorithm)
---
Steps:
1. Subdivide temperature intervals: Based on gradient stability.
2. Match temperature coefficients: Finding local linear approximations.
3. Compensate and decouple segmentally: Applying the matrix per interval.
4. Splice temperature data: Reconstructing the full temperature profile.
5. Splice phase data: Reconstructing the compensated vibration phase.
6. Compare compensation results: Final validation step.
