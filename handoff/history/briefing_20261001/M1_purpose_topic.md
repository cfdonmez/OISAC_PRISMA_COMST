## Subject, purpose, and boundaries

- **O-ISAC means Optical Integrated Sensing and Communication.** The review studies optical or photonic systems in which sensing and communication are jointly considered, integrated, designed, optimized, or evaluated within a shared architecture, link, resource framework, hardware platform, channel model, or application scenario. The live article frames the engineering problem as identifying the physical interaction and resource connecting the two functions, then tracing how a design choice changes both outcomes. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/00_baslangic/01_konu_ve_soru_formu.md:15-21; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/01_INTRODUCTION.tex:6-17; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/09_CONCLUSION.tex:4-17)
- The review spans **fiber, visible-light communication/LiFi, free-space optical links, photonics-enabled millimeter-wave/terahertz systems, hybrid optical systems, and a small “other optical” category**. The live taxonomy assigns each of 206 studies one dominant family: **69 photonic terahertz, 56 fiber, 38 VLC/LiFi, 31 free-space optical, 9 hybrid, and 3 other optical**. Physical segments and sharing mechanisms can still overlap. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/04_OPTICAL_PLATFORMS_AND_INTEGRATION_ARCHITECTURES.tex:15-20; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/04_OPTICAL_PLATFORMS_AND_INTEGRATION_ARCHITECTURES.tex:23-63)
- “Optical” does **not** mean that every target path propagates as light: photonic hardware may generate or process a carrier whose target interaction occurs over a wireless millimeter-wave or terahertz path. Fiber carrying a probe to a downstream target is also distinguished from sensing the fiber itself. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/01_INTRODUCTION.tex:19-29; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/04_OPTICAL_PLATFORMS_AND_INTEGRATION_ARCHITECTURES.tex:65-90; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/04_OPTICAL_PLATFORMS_AND_INTEGRATION_ARCHITECTURES.tex:140-150)
- The **primary evidence window is 1 January 2020 through the executed 22 June 2026 cutoff**, determined by earliest verifiable public availability. Eligible primary reports are peer-reviewed journal or early-access articles and full conference papers with sufficient **English full technical content**, a material optical/photonic component, genuine joint sensing–communication treatment, and assessable technical contribution. A bilingual report can qualify; an English abstract alone cannot. Explicit “6G” wording, two numerical outcome metrics, and a tradeoff analysis are **not mandatory**. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/09_kayitlar/checkpoints/prisma_pre_full_text_eligibility_gate_step1_2026-07-19/full_text_eligibility_criteria_LOCKED_2026-07-19.md:27-46; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/supplement/methods.md:7-11)
- Pure communication, pure sensing, RF-only ISAC without a material optical/photonic component, non-full or non-peer-reviewed items, and out-of-window reports are outside the primary corpus. Reviews, surveys, and pre-2020 foundational work can supply separately labelled context; they do not become primary technical evidence. Low reporting quality alone does not cause exclusion. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/09_kayitlar/checkpoints/prisma_pre_full_text_eligibility_gate_step1_2026-07-19/full_text_eligibility_criteria_LOCKED_2026-07-19.md:41-57; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/supplement/methods.md:9-11)
- The executed review identified **1,733 records**, assessed **272 full texts**, and retained **227 eligible reports representing 206 unique studies**: 206 primary reports plus 21 companion reports. Reports and studies are different units. The 67 contextual records from executed selection are outside the technical denominator; a later bounded positioning audit added seven contextual syntheses without changing that denominator. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/supplement/methods.md:31-47; prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/01_protokol/05_contextual_synthesis_positioning_update_2026-08-13.md:9-27)

## Review design and questions

- The protocol calls this a **“PRISMA-grounded narrative systematic review with a scoping-style PCC component.”** PCC is **Population** (peer-reviewed O-ISAC studies), **Concept** (architectures, metrics, comparability, tradeoffs, validation and benchmark readiness), and **Context** (6G-oriented optical platforms). It is not framed as a PICO intervention-effect review. The live manuscript calls it a systematic literature review reported with PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA-S. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/00_baslangic/01_konu_ve_soru_formu.md:37-81; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/01_INTRODUCTION.tex:162-175)
- The original main question asks how O-ISAC has been investigated across the five named platform groupings and what peer-reviewed work reveals about taxonomy, metric reporting and comparability, comparison admissibility, rate–sensing tradeoffs, enabling technologies, applications, validation, benchmarking, and 6G research gaps. Its **RQ1–RQ7** divide those topics into architectures/applications; metric reporting; comparison admissibility; tradeoffs; enabling methods; validation/benchmark readiness; and remaining gaps/roadmap. This is the **protocol question set**, not seven numbered questions displayed in the September article. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/00_baslangic/01_konu_ve_soru_formu.md:160-194)
- The September article expresses its purpose through **three technical contributions**: a physical basis for choosing an O-ISAC design; a common basis for interpreting communication and sensing performance under stated measurement conditions; and evidence-derived evaluation priorities using paired outcomes and separate-function baselines. Its Section VII gives **five prospective, testable questions** about resource limits, shared-reference robustness, sensing-assisted control overhead, learned functions under changed conditions, and reproducibility under service load. These proposed tests are explicitly **not additional review results**. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/01_INTRODUCTION.tex:202-233; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/08_DISCUSSION_ROADMAP_AND_LIMITATIONS.tex:25-29; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/08_DISCUSSION_ROADMAP_AND_LIMITATIONS.tex:45-81)

## Contribution, gap, and limits of interpretation

- The initial rationale was that optical platforms differ in architecture, sensing task, outcome metric, validation method, and **measurement plane**, frustrating direct cross-study comparison. The live introduction positions existing platform and broad surveys as useful, then identifies the remaining need: connect shared design choices to **both** outcomes and specify when independently reported results can inform another design. Its related-survey table distinguishes study-selection procedures from rules for cross-study comparison. It makes no supported “first” or “only” claim. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/00_baslangic/01_konu_ve_soru_formu.md:29-35; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/01_INTRODUCTION.tex:46-80; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/01_INTRODUCTION.tex:133-160; prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/07_raporlama/outputs/comst_prose_revision_2026-08-08/manuscript/comst_206_v2_9section/00_SCOPE_AND_SOURCE_MANIFEST.md)
- The synthesis is **structured narrative analysis, taxonomy and evidence mapping, condition-aware comparison, validation appraisal, and research-priority development**. Heterogeneous tasks, units, measurement planes, architectures, and validation conditions precluded a common effect measure and pooled model. No meta-analysis, meta-regression, universal performance frontier, or cross-platform ranking was performed. (prisma2020Review/systematic_review_workflow/00_baslangic/01_konu_ve_soru_formu.md:37-45; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/supplement/methods.md:85-93)
- A **joint operating point** requires a shared design setting linked to both outcomes under identified, common or explicitly alignable conditions; simultaneous operation requires further evidence that both functions operated together. A communication and sensing value merely appearing in one paper is insufficient. Cross-study numerical comparison further needs compatible tasks, metric definitions, measurement points, conditions and baselines. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/02_FOUNDATIONS_AND_COMPARISON_FRAMEWORK.tex:225-240; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/02_FOUNDATIONS_AND_COMPARISON_FRAMEWORK.tex:322-331)
- The evidence contains **4,779 primary metric records** and **404 tradeoff records**, of which **402** describe substantive relationships; these are record counts, not independent studies or experiments. The **118** recorded comparison candidates are conditional candidates, not verified independent cross-study comparisons. Twelve studies reached field/deployment settings; six reported outcomes in both domains at that level, which alone does **not** establish concurrent joint operation. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/supplement/methods.md:65-75; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/handoff/state.md:45-52; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/manuscript/sections/06_VALIDATION_REPRODUCIBILITY_AND_BENCHMARK_READINESS.tex:59-76)
- **TQAF** is the review-specific **Technical Quality Assessment Framework**: a descriptive appraisal of technical and reporting support. It is neither a conventional risk-of-bias instrument nor GRADE, and its scores have not been independently validated. PRISMA supplies a reporting framework; it does not certify conduct or evidence quality. (OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/supplement/methods.md:77-83; OISAC_PRISMA_COMST_current/governance/V3_ACTIVE_WRITING_RULES.md:89-95)
