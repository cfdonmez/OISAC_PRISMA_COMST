## C. The Fragmentation Challenge: A Landscape Without Unity

Despite the compelling physical advantages outlined above, the O-ISAC research landscape remains deeply fragmented. Our systematic analysis of **221 peer-reviewed O-ISAC studies (2020–2025)** reveals four interconnected manifestations of this fragmentation: inconsistent terminology, non-standardized sensing metrics, siloed modality communities, and weak cross-domain technology transfer. These barriers collectively impede reproducibility, cross-study comparison, and the development of a unified 6G optical sensing–communication framework.

**Terminology Proliferation.** A foundational barrier to synthesis is the profusion of synonymous and near-synonymous terms for the same underlying concept. A single paper may introduce "ISAC" as *"also known as radar-communication (RadCom), joint radar-communication (JRC), and other related terms"* [O_ISAC_161], while VLC-centric works describe "*Joint Communication and Sensing (JCS), also known as Integrated Sensing And Communication (ISAC), Sensing-Communication Integration (SCI)*" [O_ISAC_068]. In the fiber community, terminology fragments further into "ISAC-OF" (ISAC in Optical Fiber), "fiber-ISAC," and "photonic ISAC" [O_ISAC_041], [O_ISAC_033]. This aliasing is not merely cosmetic: it complicates literature searches, inflates perceived novelty, and obscures conceptual connections across modalities. Our corpus analysis identified over a dozen distinct naming conventions for optically-enabled joint sensing–communication systems.

**Metric Non-Isomorphism.** Equally problematic is the lack of standardized sensing performance metrics. While the **physical range resolution** is governed by signal bandwidth according to \(\Delta r = c/(2B)\)—a fundamental limit independent of estimator design—the literature conflates this intrinsic resolution with **SNR-dependent estimation error** (reported as RMSE, \(\sigma_r\), or "localization error") and **information-theoretic bounds** (Cramér-Rao Bound, Fisher Information Matrix) [O_ISAC_013], [O_ISAC_050], [O_ISAC_056]. Our conflict analysis reveals concrete instances of this mismatch: fiber DAS studies report "spatial resolution of 10 m" referring to the minimum resolvable segment length [O_ISAC_013], while FSO ranging papers quote "range resolution of 1.5 cm" using bandwidth-derived definitions [O_ISAC_035]. Furthermore, signal quality is inconsistently characterized—some works report optical SNR (OSNR) in the coherent domain [O_ISAC_028], while VLC studies report electrical SNR post-photodetection [O_ISAC_009]—making direct performance comparison across modalities intractable without careful normalization.

**Sub-Domain Siloing and Limited Cross-Citation.** The O-ISAC literature has evolved along four largely independent trajectories: fiber-based DAS/DSCM integration, FSO ranging–communication systems, VLC positioning–data links, and photonic-THz ISAC. Each community draws upon distinct channel models (guided vs. turbulent vs. LoS-dominated), hardware paradigms (coherent transceivers vs. IM/DD LEDs), and evaluation benchmarks—with limited cross-pollination [O_ISAC_033], [O_ISAC_050], [O_ISAC_082]. For instance, sensing–communication trade-off analyses in fiber systems emphasize phase sensitivity and strain resolution [O_ISAC_042], [O_ISAC_080], while VLC works focus on localization accuracy and gesture recognition [O_ISAC_022], [O_ISAC_062]. Photonic-THz demonstrations prioritize ultra-wide-bandwidth waveform design for simultaneous high-rate links and mm-scale ranging [O_ISAC_002], [O_ISAC_082]. Despite shared physical-layer principles—ultra-wide bandwidth enabling high resolution, optical field manipulation for beamforming—cross-modality citations remain sparse. As articulated in recent assessments, "*VLC and VLP systems are usually designed separately... mutual benefits between positioning and communication have not been utilized effectively*" [O_ISAC_039], and "*interoperability and certification across sectors remain a significant barrier*" [O_ISAC_161].

**Weak Cross-Domain Technology Transfer.** The fragmentation extends beyond terminology and metrics to the practical transfer of techniques. Waveform innovations developed for fiber ISAC (e.g., LFM-embedded training symbols [O_ISAC_042], polarization-based sensing probes [O_ISAC_074]) remain largely confined to the cabled domain, while VLC-specific solutions (multi-carrier localization via m-CAP [O_ISAC_022], federated learning for joint positioning [O_ISAC_039]) have not been adapted for FSO or Photo-THz channels. Hardware architectures differ fundamentally: coherent fiber transceivers with DSP-intensive equalization [O_ISAC_033] versus IM/DD LED-based O-ISAC with amplitude constraints [O_ISAC_054]. This heterogeneity, coupled with the absence of standardized benchmarks, evaluation scenarios, and reporting protocols, limits the portability of insights across the O-ISAC landscape [O_ISAC_068], [O_ISAC_067].

**The Missing Unifying Framework.** Collectively, these challenges point to a critical gap: the absence of a unified physical-layer taxonomy, standardized performance reporting contract, and cross-modality benchmark suite. To enable meaningful comparison and roadmapping, the O-ISAC community requires a minimal reporting standard specifying: (i) communication metrics (data rate, BER/FEC margin), (ii) ranging metrics distinguishing physical resolution (\(\Delta r\)) from estimation error (RMSE) and theoretical bounds (CRB/FIM), (iii) signal quality (electrical vs. optical SNR at specified reference points), and (iv) channel/scenario assumptions. Recent calls for "*a standard VLC channel model... following the modeling methodology of 3GPP*" [O_ISAC_327] and "*standardization efforts for facilitating interoperability*" [O_ISAC_068], [O_ISAC_082] underscore the urgency of this unification. Without such a framework, the field risks continued siloing, duplicated effort, and missed opportunities for synergistic cross-domain innovation.

*The fragmentation diagnosed above is not unique to O-ISAC—indeed, existing surveys in the broader literature reflect this same division, with fiber sensing surveys rarely addressing communication throughput, VLC positioning reviews omitting sensing–communication trade-offs, and FSO channel studies lacking ISAC perspectives.
