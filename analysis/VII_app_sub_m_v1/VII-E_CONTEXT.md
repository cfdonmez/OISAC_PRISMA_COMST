Section VII-E is locked to the `space_satellite` vertical, where O-ISAC is deployed in satellite-network operations rather than terrestrial access. In this setting, optical inter-satellite links form a communication-plane backbone for direct satellite-to-satellite transport and constellation-level traffic relaying, with explicit emphasis on throughput scaling and global service continuity [O_ISAC_089]. Deployment evidence also highlights LEO constellations, where communication-plane objectives include broad coverage and low-latency wide-area service across moving platforms [O_ISAC_089][O_ISAC_187]. At the same time, sensing-plane functionality is co-located on the same spaceborne platforms, so remote sensing can be executed alongside communication payload operation in a unified O-ISAC stack [O_ISAC_187]. Representative use-case motifs in this subsection are therefore ISL backbone networking, LEO constellation service relay, and LEO-integrated remote sensing plus data delivery under a single space deployment context [O_ISAC_089][O_ISAC_187].
