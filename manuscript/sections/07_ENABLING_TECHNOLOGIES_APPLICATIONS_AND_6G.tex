\section{APPLICATION REQUIREMENTS AND NETWORK OPERATION}
\label{sec:technologies_applications_6g}

An application determines which part of the joint operating region is useful.
A fiber monitor needs persistent observations while traffic continues; a moving
terminal needs a position or channel estimate early enough to guide the next
transmission. Both may reuse a signal, yet their limiting conditions differ.
The mechanisms in Sections~\ref{sec:optical_modality_families}
and~\ref{sec:tradeoffs} become system choices when these requirements are
specified. This section follows that progression from the optical observation
to a service outcome, using the validation distinctions in
Section~\ref{sec:validation_reproducibility} to identify what each demonstration
supports.

\subsection{Continuous Monitoring Along a Communication Path}

Fiber infrastructure provides a direct setting for sustained sharing: the
medium carrying communication also responds to the environment being sensed.
Phase, backscatter, and polarization can support distributed observations
along a route. Coherent multicore transmission with distributed sensing and
operational submarine cable monitoring illustrate this reuse at different
scales \cite{OISAC_SCR00346,OISAC_SCR00957}. Their application value depends on
preserving a useful optical observation together with traffic continuity.
Topology, launch and receiver conditions, and the disturbance context therefore
belong to the service definition, rather than being incidental laboratory
settings.

The sensing task changes the required observation and reference. A fiber
platform for vehicles combines high-rate data transmission with distributed
temperature measurement \cite{OISAC_SCR00123}. Infrastructure diagnosis can
instead process collected fiber data into event classes
\cite{OISAC_SCR01006}. Temperature estimates and event labels place different
demands on calibration, observation duration, and reference data. An application
evaluation should connect those demands to the communication interval affected
by sensing. Offline classification can demonstrate information in a recorded
signal, while timely operational diagnosis additionally depends on acquisition,
processing, and delivery of the result.

Sharing extends beyond the sensing fiber when observations must be transported.
One study relates transport capacity to the number of sensing streams meeting
an echo-quality threshold \cite{OISAC_SCR00436}; another connects sensing traffic
capacity with target count and resolution requirements \cite{OISAC_SCR01049}.
These cases make a system-level coupling explicit: acquiring richer sensing
observations creates traffic that consumes network resources. The useful
operating point depends on the observations that reach the processing stage
with sufficient quality, alongside the communication service maintained by the
same infrastructure.

\subsection{Mobility, Directional Links, and the Age of an Estimate}

For a directional wireless link, sensing can inform where energy should be
sent. A distributed W-band system uses radar estimates for beam alignment and
flexible data service, while microcomb synchronization coordinates wireless
data with radar imaging \cite{OISAC_SCR00502,OISAC_SCR00406}. These examples link
spatial information and a shared reference to communication operation. Their
service interpretation depends on the complete interval from observation to
transmission: the geometry at acquisition may differ from the geometry when a
beam or resource decision is applied.

Observation age creates a pointing cost even when estimation error is small.
Consider a sequential steering loop using an estimate referenced to the start
of acquisition. Its age at actuation is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{age}}=T_{\mathrm{acq}}+T_{\mathrm{proc}}
+T_{\mathrm{transport}}+T_{\mathrm{act}},
\label{eq:observation_age}
\end{equation}
where the terms are acquisition, processing, transport, and actuation times.
For constant relative transverse velocity $v_\perp$, nearly fixed distance $d$,
and small angular displacement, uncompensated motion contributes
\begin{equation}
\Delta\theta_{\mathrm{motion}}\simeq
\frac{|v_\perp|T_{\mathrm{age}}}{d}.
\label{eq:motion_pointing}
\end{equation}
The remaining pointing budget must accommodate this displacement together
with other errors at the selected service threshold. These illustrative
kinematic relations explain why a precise but stale estimate can fail to
support a narrow beam; they are not fitted results from the corpus.
An overlapped pipeline uses the actual interval between the estimate's
reference time and actuation. Prediction, acceleration, and measurement
uncertainty require their own residual-error models.

The optical phased-array and frequency-comb demonstrations discussed earlier
connect steering with ranging or imaging
\cite{OISAC_SCR00941,OISAC_SCR00362}. In a moving application, narrower beams
make acquisition, tracking, blockage, and calibration consequential for both
functions. A useful evaluation therefore follows rate or reliability alongside
sensing error and update timing as the geometry changes. A static alignment
result identifies an achievable operating point; motion and recovery tests
establish the conditions over which that point remains useful.

The steering mechanism must also remain physically explicit. The optical
intelligent-surface study models a controlled mobile FSO path
\cite{OISAC_SCR00914}, whereas a laboratory demonstration uses mechanically
rotated Risley prisms to steer a photonically generated terahertz signal
\cite{OISAC_SCR00953}. Both redirect energy, but the control variables,
propagation physics, and validation settings differ. Their future comparison
requires a shared task and stated motion conditions, with actuator behavior
and conversion interfaces retained in the timing budget.

\subsection{Indoor Operation, Human Use, and Difficult Environments}

In indoor optical systems, the user or robot's position and orientation shape
both received power and the sensing geometry. Multicarrier positioning and
robotic communication illustrate different consequences of this dependence
\cite{OISAC_SCR01024,OISAC_SCR00910}. The robotics study reports packet loss over
distance and view angle, display delay over queue size, and formation control
under dynamic motion as separate results \cite{OISAC_SCR00910}. Together they
show why communication reliability, information freshness, and control behavior
need distinct measurements. They should not be collapsed into a single paired
operating point unless their conditions and timing are linked by the source.

Human-facing optical devices connect communication or remote alerts with
motion, falls, air pressure, physiology, or gesture
\cite{OISAC_SCR00451,OISAC_SCR00294,OISAC_SCR00376}. Here, a sensing estimate is
useful when it represents the intended event or physiological quantity under
the user's motion and illumination conditions. Communication is useful when
that information can be delivered with the required continuity. Reference
measurements, event definitions, and processing delay consequently matter
alongside link rate. This requirement also distinguishes a recognition result
on collected data from an evaluated sensing-and-alert service.

Extreme environments impose a different set of physical constraints.
Satellite-link work connects communication with atmospheric or wavefront
sensing \cite{OISAC_SCR00456}, making pointing and propagation part of the
adaptation problem. Underwater optical-link concepts and subsea fiber
monitoring involve different paths \cite{OISAC_SCR00763,OISAC_SCR01095}.
Attenuation through water applies to the former; cable calibration and access
to the installed infrastructure constrain the latter. Grouping both by their
application environment is useful for locating requirements, but their
physical observations and attainable operating regions remain distinct.

\subsection{Inference and Control as Parts of the Joint System}

Learning methods convert optical observations into positions, event labels,
field estimates, channel predictions, or resource decisions. Fiber diagnosis,
federated visible-light positioning, and wearable optical sensing illustrate
this range \cite{OISAC_SCR01006,OISAC_SCR01081,OISAC_SCR00376}. The inference task
adds a reference-data requirement to the physical measurement: reported
accuracy depends on what was labeled, how data were divided, and the conditions
represented in evaluation. Applying a learned model in another layout or
operating regime therefore introduces a further test of the joint system.

One coherent-link model both compensates transmission impairments and
estimates distributed power and abnormal loss \cite{OISAC_SCR00949}. The
outputs share a link, but retain different targets and reference information.
This architecture motivates testing whether a change in the link affects the
two outputs similarly, and whether adaptation that helps one preserves the
other. The answer cannot be inferred from communication compensation or
sensing accuracy alone.

Processing also consumes time and resources. Classifier size changes
recognition accuracy and the edge-processing burden in
\cite{OISAC_SCR01047}. A digital-twin study represents vehicular edge resources
and relates orthogonal task channels to application latency
\cite{OISAC_SCR01059}. Another uses a model-aided physical reference for coherent
fiber sensing \cite{OISAC_SCR01185}. These model roles differ: one organizes
resources for a service, while the other supports interpretation of the
physical observation. Reporting the represented state, update process, model
mismatch, and resulting decision connects either role to a testable system
benefit. It also allows inference accuracy to be weighed against the delay and
resource use needed to obtain it.

\subsection{From a Subsystem Benefit to a Network Role}

Table~\ref{tab:application_requirements} connects these applications through
the evidence needed to sustain a useful service. At network scale, an O-ISAC
subsystem must occupy an explicit role in access, fronthaul, transport, edge
processing, or service control. The consequences of sharing then include
traffic, timing, orchestration, energy, and recovery across interfaces. For
example, channel sensing can inform access placement under weather and demand
changes \cite{OISAC_SCR00922}; evaluating its system benefit requires connecting
the sensed condition to the placement decision and resulting communication
service.

The corpus includes studies with an explicit 6G role
\cite{OISAC_SCR00362,OISAC_SCR01049} and enabling contributions that require
additional network context \cite{OISAC_SCR00007,OISAC_SCR00647}. The complete
relevance classification and application inventory are provided in the
electronic supplement. These classifications describe the literature's
connection to 6G. Standard conformance, interoperability, and deployment
readiness require evidence tied to the relevant network framework
\cite{ITUR_M2160_2023}.

The remaining engineering question is whether the observed subsystem benefit
persists after including the resources used to acquire, transport, process,
and act on sensing information. Answering it requires the same two functional
outcomes examined earlier, now measured under the load, motion, and timing of
the intended role. Those unresolved links define the research tests developed
in Section~\ref{sec:discussion_roadmap}.
