\section{RESEARCH QUESTIONS AND EVALUATION PRIORITIES}
\label{sec:discussion_roadmap}

The preceding sections connect a physical sensing interaction to a shared
design choice, two performance outcomes, and the conditions of an application.
The unresolved issue is how far a useful relation persists when those
conditions change. Greater integration can improve coordination, while also
coupling distortion, synchronization, dynamic range, and failure behavior.
Research can resolve this tension by identifying the mechanism that limits
joint operation and testing the variables expected to move that limit.

\subsection{From Reported Relations to Discriminating Experiments}

The literature offers recurring mechanisms across optical platforms, but their
quantitative consequences belong to particular tasks and measurement settings
\cite{Wen2024OISACArchitectures,Su2025OISACTheoryPractice,
Khorasgani2026GeneralOpticalISACSurvey}. A useful next experiment should
therefore begin with a relation already observed within a study. It should
state which explanation is being tested, change the relevant parameter, and
measure both functions against suitable separate-function baselines. The
shared-resource budget must remain explicit: increasing a sensing probe with
communication launch power held fixed answers a different question from
redistributing a fixed total power budget.

Table~\ref{tab:research_roadmap} identifies five research questions arising from
the reviewed mechanisms and validation limits. The proposed tests distinguish
an achievable operating point from the range of conditions over which that
point remains useful. They are prospective experiments, not additional results
of the review.

These experiments require perturbations that act on the native signal path.
Fiber tests can vary topology, traffic, or the sensed disturbance; VLC tests
can vary illumination and orientation; FSO tests can vary pointing and
atmosphere. Photonic terahertz tests should separate optical drift from changes
in wireless propagation before combining them. This separation helps explain
whether the observed loss of joint performance follows from the proposed
coupling mechanism or from a different component. Repeated paired trials
then show the variability of the operating region under the stated conditions.

The result remains useful if integration provides no net gain. For example,
if sensing-assisted alignment improves a static link but its update delay
erases the advantage during motion, the crossover defines when the control
method is appropriate. Likewise, a shared reference that improves one output
while destabilizing the other identifies a need for compensation, a different
allocation, or partial separation. Such findings turn a performance comparison
into a design rule with an explicit range of validity.

\subsection{Building Comparable Tests Across Groups and Networks}

Independent comparisons need a common task within a physical modality, a
reference design, calibration and ground truth, and sufficient artifacts to
recover the reported processing. Agreement should concern the relative result
and its conditions, with model bounds and empirical measurements retaining
their different meanings. Such a test supplies evidence of transfer through
an independently executed comparison.

At network scale, the experiment also needs the load, timing, and resource
use of the intended role. Transporting observations, updating a model, or
steering a beam can consume the resources that create the apparent subsystem
benefit. Fielded cable and network work illustrates the importance of
operational continuity \cite{Cao2026SubmarineFiberISAC,Ip2026DeployedTelecomCableISAC}.
Future tests can connect that continuity to the sensing decision and include
recovery from a component failure or corrupted observation. The resulting
benchmarks would support discussion of network requirements while preserving
the propagation and measurement conditions of each platform.

\subsection{Scope and Limitations of the Review}

The publication window, language requirements, and search sources in
Section~\ref{sec:review_methods} delimit the literature represented here.
Full texts were assessed for 272 of 330 sought reports, or 82.4\%; findings
from unavailable reports may be absent. Selection involved investigator
judgment without routine independent duplicate assessment. The scientific
methods supplement documents these limitations and the review conduct.

TQAF is a review-specific appraisal that has not been independently validated.
Hybrid-system classification and thematic synthesis also involve judgment.
Differences in tasks, metrics, measurement planes, and source protocols
prevented a common pooled effect model. No meta-regression, formal
publication-bias assessment, or formal sensitivity analysis was performed.
The synthesis therefore supports interpretation of reported mechanisms and
conditions, rather than a universal effect, platform ranking, or estimate of
worldwide technological maturity. These limits bound the proposed research
priorities to the gaps identified in the retained literature.
