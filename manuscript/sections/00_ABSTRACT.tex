\begin{abstract}
Optical integrated sensing and communication (O-ISAC) reuses optical
infrastructure, signals, or hardware to transmit data and observe a physical
environment. Its implementations span guided fiber, optical wireless, and
photonics-enabled millimeter-wave and terahertz links, whose different sensing
paths complicate the interpretation of reported performance. This systematic
review synthesizes 227 eligible reports representing 206 studies published
from January 2020 through June 2026. We connect each architecture's shared
resource to the design variable, physical mechanism, and communication and
sensing outcomes it affects, retaining source-specific measurement and
validation conditions. The synthesis identifies distinct routes to joint
performance: reference allocation can reduce payload opportunity; increased
probe power can improve sensing while impairing data recovery; and interference
suppression or sensing-guided steering can support communication without the
same resource exchange. These mechanisms explain why separately reported peak
rates and sensing errors cannot define a common performance frontier. Laboratory
and prototype evidence is broad, but only 12 studies reach field or deployment
settings, with six reporting outcomes in both functional domains at that level.
That coverage alone does not establish concurrent joint operation. We derive
application-specific tests of traffic continuity, motion, optical drift, and
inference cost, using paired measurements and separate-function baselines to
determine when a demonstrated subsystem benefit remains useful in a network.
\end{abstract}
