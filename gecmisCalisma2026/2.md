## Abstract

Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) is viewed as a powerful component of future mobile networks and has gained considerable attention from both academic and industrial experts. This promising technology aims to offer both high-resolution sensing/ranging and large-capacity wireless communication simultaneously. Similar to the concept of radio-frequency (RF) ISAC, the integration between optical communication and optical sensing yields optical-ISAC (O-ISAC), which is regarded as a strong complement to and augment of its RF counterpart. O-ISAC technology can effectively reduce the stringent requirements of spectrum and hardware with efficient resource utilization while simultaneously performing high-accuracy sensing and high-capacity communication. In this review, we first elaborate on the generalized system of O-ISAC in light of existing contributions, recent advances, and then explain different categories of O-ISAC in the context of photonics, visible light communication, free space optics, and optical fiber technologies. We also outline the role of promising distributed optical fiber sensing and its coexistence with joint waveform designs. To further highlight scientific efforts on this topic, this paper presents a comprehensive overview of several experimental works from the literature. Also, we elaborate on the appealing benefits of O-ISAC such as maximizing communication rate, improving sensing capability, and reducing interference. Next, waveform designs including pulsed waveform, constant-modulus waveform, and multi-carrier waveform are discussed. Furthermore, advanced hardware designs, hybrid designs, performance metrics, modulation schemes, multi-beamforming, and resource-multiplexing are discussed as key fundamentals of robust and scalable O-ISAC systems. Additionally, machine learning and deep learning solutions are explored for efficient resource allocation. To indicate the prospects in future intelligent and multifunctional applications, we also highlight some application scenarios of O-ISAC in terms of Internet-of-things networks, quantum communication networks, aerial networks, cooperative mobile robotics, smart healthcare, toxic or corrosive gas leakage monitoring, smart bridges, and vehicular networks. In the end, we put forward open research issues and future perspectives, which are envisioned to offer some novel directions for future research. We believe this study will present the vision of paramount and innovative aspects of O-ISAC technology for both academic and industrial progress.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1. Overview

RF-ISAC has attracted growing research interest in recent years, with many system models and prototypes having been introduced. Generally, it is the mainstream solution for wide coverage, yet the inherent drawback of spectrum congestion and the stringent requirement for highprecision sensing have stimulated more research interest towards optical technologies. Benefiting from the huge spectral bandwidth, optical ISAC (O-ISAC) links can provide ultra-high data rates of gigabits per second. The shorter wavelength of optical beams empowers more precise distance measurements and target localization, which can be further improved through the synergy of promising technologies like light

detection and ranging (LiDAR). The high optical beam diversity also offers significant sensing gains. O-ISAC systems can provide highly refined environmental perception capabilities as compared to RF-ISAC [1,2]. Under feasible sampling and aperture conditions, O-ISAC can achieve microradian-level angular resolution and centimeter-level range resolution. Furthermore, the high directionality of optical beams also enables precise sensing and transmission through beamforming methods, efficiently mitigating interference. Moreover, by leveraging the flexible control and line-of-sight (LoS) propagation of optical beams, O-ISAC can efficiently suppress interference from unwanted sources. Furthermore, optical communication, particularly visible light communication (VLC), can be seamlessly incorporated with existing lighting infrastructures such as light emitting diode (LED) to conceive various functionalities, including communication, coarse-grained sensing,

These appealing advantages highlight the compelling potential of O-ISAC and make it specifically suitable for meeting the ultra-high throughput requirements of 6G. In terms of integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), the optical band shows striking resemblance to the radio frequency (RF) band. As RF-ISAC continues to accelerate, the optical-band-based ISAC emerges as a promising alternative [1]. First, well-established predominant RF-ISAC waveforms like orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) and linear frequency modulation (LFM) can be transformed into optical waveforms, empowering optical devices like LEDs and laser diodes (LDs) to offer both active sensing and communication capabilities simultaneously. Second, both optical sensing and optical communication can use the intensity-modulation and direct-detection (IM/DD) scheme, ensuring shared baseband units and optical front ends. Third, the principles of data sharing and joint optimization apply to both optical and RF waveforms, through which cooperation between sensing and communication can be conceived. However, several differences still exist between RF and optical counterparts. Particularly, the distinct features of optical channels and front-ends provide key potentials for sensing and communication; thus, it can serve as a promising complement to RF-ISAC [2]. Consequently, the concept of O-ISAC emerges with the coexistence of optical sensing and optical communication. Leveraging the inherent advantages of short wavelength, immunity to RF interference, and expansive unlicensed bandwidth of the optical spectrum, O-ISAC offers the capability to support Gbps-level communication and centimeter-level precise sensing [3,4]. With its blend of high-precision sensing and high-speed communication, O-ISAC is envisaged to augment and complement its RF counterpart [5]. In O-ISAC, the multiplexing of sensing and communication can further improve the band utility of the system, offer powerful dual-functionality which cannot be conceived through a single VLC or visible light positioning (VLP) system [2,6]. Despite these benefits, traditional O-ISAC systems still depend on natural reflections from surrounding surfaces like ceilings, walls, and other physical objects. Under such conditions, the stability and strength of the echo signal are largely constrained by the surface geometry and material, which often leads to strong sensitivity and weak returns due to roughness and color. It must be emphasized that spatial resolution is the only characteristic that determines the sensing performance. Noise level and echo quality jointly determine the achievable signal-to-noise ratio, which directly impacts the localization and ranging accuracy [7]. Under these practical constraints, achieving reliable and stable sensing over large detection distances is critically challenging. ## 2. Categories of O-ISAC

O-ISAC encompasses various deployment methods and spectral ranges, generally classified into VLC, FSO, and photonic sensing, as depicted in Fig. 2. These categories indicate the applicability of optical technologies for joint sensing and communication tasks. Visible light communication in O-ISAC: VLC uses LEDs as transmitters and imaging sensors or photodetectors (PDs) as receivers to achieve environmental sensing and data transmission simultaneously. In ISAC applications, VLC can perform environmental mapping, motion tracking, indoor positioning and localization while serving as a highbandwidth communication medium. ## 3. Optical-fiber-based ISAC

With the appealing benefits of high interference immunity, large bandwidth, and low loss, optical fibers are extensively implemented for data transmission in several applications. To date, optical fiber communication has become the most prominent technology contributing above 90% of global data transmissions [39]. Apart from applicability in data transmissions, optical fibers have been used for sensing purposes as well [40]. Fiber optic sensing technology uses transmission characteristics of optical beams to detect physical quantities or calculate environmental parameters. It enables remote monitoring of optical signals and high sensitivity [41]. It can empower high-precision sensing even in uneven or harsh scenarios like radiation, underwater, and high temperature. As the need for data transmission continues to expand, the use of fiber optic technology is growing at an unprecedented rate. Furthermore, vast-scale implementation of fiber optic technology also needs high-precision monitoring. Consequently, it has emerged as a notable research domain to realize real-time monitoring and sensing of optical fibers during data transmissions. It can ensure precise tracking of optical fibers to avoid any disruptions such as damage by sharks in underwater environments. Fiber-optic based ISAC technology has become an interesting research topic and several efforts have been made in the literature [42].

## 4. System architecture, waveform designs and advantages

O-ISAC integrates optical sensing and communication in a unified system architecture, where the promising advantages of enhanced communication rate, enhanced sensing precision, and reduced multiuser interference (MUI) can be achieved. Next, we briefly discuss the system architecture and advantages in detail.

### 4.1. System architecture

as reported in earlier studies. At the transmitting end, the communication signal is encoded and modulated before integration with the sensing signal to design the O-ISAC signal. After that, the O-ISAC signal is DCbiased and transmitted using optical source e.g., an LD in the free space channel. The O-ISAC signal propagates in the free space atmospheric channel and is hampered by ambient light, turbulence, and attenuation. The atmospheric turbulence and attenuation are generally considered at the receiver side, while the ambient light can be mitigated through photon coincidence detection or a closed-loop circuit [73]. Moreover, the target also reflects some part of the O-ISAC signal, whose spatial information is integrated into the reflected signal and recovered by the sensing receiver. At the receiving end, analog-to-digital converter and PD are common modules shared by the sensing and communication receivers, followed by the pre-processing modules to nullify ambient light and amplify received signal. Essential characteristics such as the velocity, distance, and time of flight (ToF), can be obtained by the sensing receiver, and the communication receiver can perform synchronization, demodulation and decoding. If the communication modules are omitted, the operating principle of O-ISAC resembles a laser radar. Thus, deploying O-ISAC on a laser radar or optical sensing devices is more appealing than establishing a new system. Furthermore, large antenna arrays in RF-ISAC can be replaced with LDs and PDs to develop an O-ISAC system.

### 4.2. Advantages

### 4.3. Waveform design for O-ISAC

Maximizing communication rate: In contrast to the congested RF spectrum, the optical spectrum provides a wide unlicensed spectrum for high data rate communication. Moreover, benefitting from the promising coherence, collimation, and monochromaticity of laser beams, multiple data streams can be transmitted in parallel using multiplexing schemes such as mode division multiplexing, FDM and WDM, through which communication rate can be further improved [27]. Improving sensing capability: As extensively used optical sensors, laser radars have the potential of recognition, imaging, ranging, and detection. Compared to RF counterparts, laser radars can ensure more delicate spatial information and highly sophisticated resolution under the same operating mechanism, owing to their small divergence angle and wide bandwidth of laser beams. These features can significantly enhance the sensing capability of O-ISAC with higher distance and angular resolution than those of RF-ISAC. However, a higher angular resolution also needs more sophisticated acquisition, pointing, and tracking (APT) mechanism, which poses more stringent demands for both signal processing and hardware architecture. Reducing interference: Another critical task for ISAC systems is to cancel interference between sensing and communication. The optical spectrum offers even narrow beams as compared to mmWave of the same aperture, and therefore different access points and users can be separated accurately in the spatial domain. Moreover, O-ISAC typically relies on LoS channels, which mitigates the interference as optical beams cannot penetrate opaque obstructions. Meanwhile, beamforming and beam steering methods can be adapted to mitigate interference. Thus, OISAC yields a superior anti-interference ability. By dynamically controlling the directivity of the laser beams, O-ISAC can mitigate interference and precisely focus beams on targets. Most of the existing beam steering techniques are expensive, fragile, and bulky, thus novel techniques must be introduced to ensure superior sensing and communication performance of O-ISAC systems.

Another important element for ISAC is waveform design, which emphasizes developing a unified waveform to support sensing and communication functionalities simultaneously. Extensive research has been carried out on waveform design for RF-ISAC, including nonoverlapped resource allocation and fully joint waveform strategies. However, an O-ISAC waveform based on IM/DD scheme is limited to being non-negative and real, and therefore existing RF-ISAC waveforms cannot be directly applied to O-ISAC due to their complex-valued nature. To tackle this challenge, several strategies have been introduced to make RF-ISAC waveforms compatible with O-ISAC, such as multi-carrier waveform, constant-modulus waveform, and pulsed waveform. Subsequently, resource allocation and waveform optimization can be performed to secure global optimal waveform design. Pulsed waveform: The pulsed waveform is highly embraced by both optical sensing and communication owing to its cost-effective implementation and high energy efficiency. Pulse position modulation (PPM), that encodes communication signals onto pulses in particular binary slots, is validated to be optimal in the context of energy efficiency. Additionally, distance information can be collected from the ToF of pulses as they reflect from the target. Since the positioning of pulse holds the information about target distance and communication data, an OISAC scheme based on PPM is promising as depicted in Fig. 10(a). In order to obtain a non-negative and real optical signal, DC-bias is applied to ensure that negative component is not clipped. Constant-modulus waveform tends to be more energyefficient than non-constant-modulus waveforms like QAM and amplitude modulation (AM). An example of such waveform is the integration of LFM and CPM as depicted in Fig. 10(b). ## 5. Key fundamentals of O-ISAC

Advanced hardware designs, novel performance metrics, hybrid systems, and new modulation schemes are key fundamentals of O-ISAC systems, while promising DL approaches can also be integrated to ensure a reliable and robust system as depicted in Fig. 11.

### 5.2. Performance metrics

Owing to LoS channels and narrow beams, the received signal at the sensing receiver of an O-ISAC typically appears from the reflection of a point target. Such differences lead to incompatibility of existing performance metrics devoted to multi-target sensing cases, such as integrated-side lobe ratio and peak-to-side lobe ratio. In addition, another urgent task for O-ISAC is to establish an information-theorycentric foundation, which can ensure a unified performance metric through joint optimization problem formulation. Herein, the critical intricacy lies in nonlinear distortions of optical signals, which can lead to a complicated problem formulation of performance metrics [76].

### 5.3. Hybrid RF and optical design

Even though O-ISAC showcases strong interference suppression capabilities, it is still particularly prone to obstructions that lead to dead zones, misalignment and pointing errors which can be mitigated through RF-ISAC. Furthermore, since IM/DD scheme-aided O-ISAC is unable to extract velocity information directly, delay-Doppler analysis empowers RF-ISAC to sense several mobile targets simultaneously. Thus, both RF-ISAC and O-ISAC are strongly correlated, leading to a hybrid architecture as depicted in Fig. 11 to benefit from capabilities of both systems. Research has focused on hybrid systems that integrate both RF and optical systems to achieve enhanced scalability, coverage, and performance trade-offs. Hybrid systems incorporate the capabilities of both technologies: RF-ISAC delivers enhanced robustness and broader coverage in NLoS scenarios, while O-ISAC offers high bandwidth and precision in indoor and LoS conditions. A hybrid system can provide an essential backbone link if any link, either RF or optical is not working. OISAC systems can compensate for the shortcomings of RF-ISAC in scenarios including limited spectrum and high electromagnetic interference. For example, if optical link is impacted by atmospheric turbulence, then RF link can be activated to continue sensing and communication tasks without any disruptions. In addition, both systems can be developed in parallel transmission with bandwidth and power resources allocated to both O-ISAC and RF-ISAC systems. ### 5.6. Multi-domain resource multiplexing

ISAC has emerged as a potential key enabling technology for the Internet of everything (IoE) in future generation networks, empowering several transformative applications including high-precision target localization for multi-static communications, environment mapping, and tracking UAV swarms. For these application scenarios, the generation and processing of wide-bandwidth, high frequency ISAC signals are crucially necessary to provide high-resolution sensing and high-capacity communication functionalities. Nevertheless, conventional electronic techniques are vulnerable to several challenges due to electronic bottlenecks including high transmission loss in coaxial cables, high cost, narrow bandwidth, and low frequency. These shortcomings hinder the real-time applicability of ISAC systems, necessitating alternative solutions. Alternatively, the expansive deployment of microwave photonics (MWP) techniques has become a viable solution to overcome these limitations [84]. With distinct benefits including strong immunity to electromagnetic interference, fast analog signal processing, flat frequency response, low frequency-dependent loss, large instantaneous bandwidth, and high operating frequency, MWP techniques have demonstrated an unprecedented ability to achieve ultra-high-resolution sensing and extremely high-capacity mmWave/THz communication.

#### 5.8.1. Machine-learning-based eavesdropping detection

Security and privacy of both communication and sensing data are crucial for any ISAC system. In existing works, several methods have been proposed to mitigate external attacks or data leakage as eavesdropping is the most common attack in optical fiber communication. As a typical eavesdropping technique, splitting eavesdroppers can impact physical parameters of the optical fiber channel, which can be closely monitored to precisely locate the eavesdropping positions. In the case of a small splitting ratio, it is harder to detect the changes in these physical parameters and discern the negative impacts. In this context, ML is a viable approach to observe the small differences between samples and detect the impact of those small disturbances. With the potential to precisely analyze any changes in communication or sensing data, ML tools can efficiently process these physical-layer parameters. The current detection schemes for optical fiber eavesdropping usually include two categories. The first category is eavesdropping detection based on physical properties such as polarization, phase, etc. The most common example is DOFS with the sensing fiber [94]. DOFS offers a sound or vibration technique for security monitoring using the Φ-OTDR method. Since the physical properties of the optical fiber are sensitive to vibrations in the surrounding environment, it can lead to false information. The second category is based on monitoring optical transmission performance for eavesdropping detection. Since the power of optical signal is an important parameter, it can reflect the signal loss due to potential eavesdropping impact. In Ref. [95], the authors introduced a detection strategy based on average optical power. In the proposed system, an alarm signal was triggered when the average optical power changed from a given reference value. Nevertheless, the premise of the proposed strategy is an encoding method, which is complex and limits it to low transmission efficiency. Similarly, the OTDR is a common mechanism to monitor and maintain optical fiber by measuring the reduction in signal power [96]. However, it is typically utilized to identify the fault points in network fault such as fiber cut. Since eavesdropping can be performed with a small loss in signal power, OTDR is unable to effectively detect eavesdropping. The emergence of ML technology offers an innovative way for eavesdropping detection in optical fiber. ML has been extensively utilized in the optical physical layer to efficiently handle uncertainty and uncertain information [97], such as transmission quality estimation [98], optical amplifier control [99], fiber nonlinearity compensation [100], modulation format identification [101], and optical performance monitoring [102]. In recent studies, ML has been implemented for security diagnostics in optical networks. In Ref. [103], the authors experimentally investigated an ML framework to detect and identify in-band and out-band polarization scrambling attacks and jamming signal insertion attacks. In Ref. [104], the authors performed investigation and recognition of Optical Spectrum Feature Analysis (OSFA) for physical layer attacks. However, the proposed system only focuses on the detection of malicious attacks in the optical network but does not address eavesdropping in the end-to-end system. Several studies have focused on investigating the effect of physical parameters on channel characteristics of optical fiber. The authors in Ref. [105] developed an analytical model of coherent optical OFDM (CO-OFDM) by combining the impacts of dispersion and amplified spontaneous emission (ASE) noise and four-wave mixing (FWM) scheme. Furthermore, a closed-form analytical expression was formulated in Ref. [106] for the nonlinear system performance of CO-OFDM systems. Hence, some physical properties of optical fiber channel are highly sensitive to attacks, failure, and eavesdropping. In simple words, the eavesdropping in optical fiber can be distinguished by analyzing and observing the variations in physical metrics. In a relevant work, the authors proposed ML algorithms to tackle the optical performance by monitoring eye diagrams and data. To enhance the accuracy and efficiency of eavesdropping detection, both eyediagrams and optical performance monitoring (OPM) are used opted as input data. ## 7. Open research issues and future aspects

In this section, we have highlighted critical issues which pose limitations on the performance and deployment of this technology and future research directions to overcome these critical issues and further advance this technology. Here, we have discussed different challenges in O-ISAC systems in detail. • Multi-parameter sensing: Most of the existing reported O-ISAC systems can only perform sensing of vibrations during transmission. It imposes a critical limit to integrate different sensing objects where vibration is not the key constraint or where different physical parameters are essential to be monitored. For instance, multiplexed sensing fibers are utilized to obtain dual-parameter distributed sensing, calculating strain and temperature simultaneously [12]. In such cases, the designed systems for vibrations will not be practical anymore. Thus, novel systems based on multi-parameters sensing are needed to overcome this issue. • Balance trade-off: For the O-ISAC system integrated with DOFS, the optimal balance of the trade-off between sensing and communication performance must be considered. The nonlinearity due to DOFS has puts a substantial impact on the communication performance. Some recent works have addressed this issue. The authors in Ref. [9] proposed and designed the integration of DOFS with joint waveform to significantly decrease the nonlinearity impact on the communication performance. Thus, the research fraternity should explore novel joint waveforms that can enhance both sensing and communication performance. • Advanced optical techniques: With the aim of breaking the limitation of conventional optical fiber systems, a considerable amount of effort has been devoted to enhance the sensing performance of DOFS by adopting different optical communication techniques, and significant achievements have been made, which opens a new research direction to improve the performance of DOFS. However, these attempts are only made considering simple optical communication detection techniques for distributed sensing. Recently, a notable interest has been made in considering advanced optical communication techniques to empower sensing functionality. Specifically, it is highly essential to explore the applicability and feasibility of extracting sensing data directly from an integrated optical communication system. Since this is an untouched research domain, further investigation should be made for possible integration of advanced optical communication systems with distributed sensing. • Detection techniques: SDM using a few modes or multicore fiber has been widely explored in the literature. It has shown promising potential for distributed optical sensing systems, where detection of various parameters empowers innovative sensing applications.

O-ISAC systems frequently depend on DNNs, ML, RL, and FL, each introducing distinct benefits and limitations [166]. In particular, DNNs are efficient at capturing nonlinear, complex relationships and have shown state-of-the-art potential in tasks like joint waveform optimization, channel estimation, and target detection without any need for explicit signal models. However, their dependency on significant computational resources and largely labeled training data can pose limitations for real-time adaptation and on-device training in resource-constrained O-ISAC environments. Similarly, ML tools are advancing at an unprecedented pace, and have been implemented in diverse applications due to their exceptional performance in target recognition, accurate sensing, and data processing. ML can be integrated with O-ISAC and LiSAC systems in various perspectives such as modulation design, to reduce computational complexity, channel modelling, and positioning accuracy enhancement [2,167]. RL methods also demonstrate high significance in dynamic environments—such as underwater networks, UAV operations or vehicle-to-infrastructure networks—where interference patterns and channel conditions vary rapidly. While dealing with the environment and optimizing reward functions, RL agents can perform adaptive beamforming and resource allocation strategies, yielding a robust performance under dynamic situations. However, RL's typical slow convergence and complexity of policies transfer from simulation to real-world practical developments are critical challenges that need to be carefully tackled. Similarly, FL provides a compelling alternative to preserve privacy and reduce sensing or communication overhead. It enables collaborative training method to ensure robustness and reliable convergence. However, it is critically vulnerable to synchronization delays and heterogeneous client capabilities, which require further research investigations.

• Harsh conditions: Since optical signals play a key fundamental role in O-ISAC systems, future works must consider challenges like misalignment, pointing error, obstructions and atmospheric turbulence which significantly impact the performance of optical signals. Future works should devote efforts to reducing the impact of these harsh conditions. For example, future O-ISAC systems should reduce positioning errors, while considerably restricting the number of calibration points currently needed, particularly by selecting a more robust and realistic RRS model along with ML or DL approaches to precisely determine the error correction points. Furthermore, it will be more interesting and practical for future applications to switch from 2D to 3D sensing positioning in O-ISAC systems. • Multi-beamformers: Several notable works have been reported on the multi-beamformers to achieve high-quality, broadband beamforming for O-ISAC systems. However, several shortcomings are encountered, such as the relatively wide bandwidth and the limited number of receiving channels, which cause problems like inter-beam crosstalk. Additionally, the operational frequency range or antenna elements and filters impose a considerable constraint on the total performance of the multi-beamformer, thereby restricting both the bandwidth of waveforms like LFM and the transmission rate of the carrier such a QAM signal. Moving forward, ML and DL tools can iteratively optimize the broadband multi-beamformer to overcome these shortcomings and further improve the performance of O-ISAC systems. The ongoing and future research efforts to consider these learning approaches will empower viable solutions for highperformance communication and sensing in the futuristic O-ISAC systems in B5G/6G networks. • Channel characteristics: The channel characteristics of any O-ISAC system play a crucial role. Thus, it is essential to investigate optimization strategies to enhance the accuracy of locating and detection as well the elimination of interference. Researchers should explore the sensitivity factor of channel characteristics due to external factors like physical layer eavesdropping, pressure, temperature, etc. Thus, it is an interesting research direction to realize an O-ISAC system considering these channel characteristics. • Realistic models: Another critical research gap lies in realistic timevarying O-ISAC channel models that accurately describe how different environmental perturbations cause variations in channel parameters. Several existing works take simple assumptions that environmental disturbance only impacts rotation elements due to the gap in realistic channel models. Furthermore, different deployment scenarios also lack associated channel models that define the environmental effect on the channel characteristics of underlying optical fiber. Designing new channel models therefore urges for novel datadriven algorithms, ML-aided channel estimation techniques, polarization-aided O-ISAC systems, and innovative approaches to accurately assess the performance tradeoffs of channel parameters. • Resource allocation: Looking at the complexity of existing models, it is necessary to develop innovative models to adapt to resourcesconstrained O-ISAC hardware components. In this context, hybrid optical wireless designs and ML tools can efficiently learn from OISAC data and enhance the accuracy of channel estimation, processing of sensing data and beam prediction tasks. However, the convergence of quantum communication in 6G networks poses several critical challenges such as the need to effectively manage decoherence, quantum noise and error correction mechanisms. Moreover, incorporating such advanced quantum technologies with traditional systems requires sophisticated hybrid model and upgraded hardware components compatible to handle dynamic nature and high complexity of future networks. Thus, addressing these challenges is crucial for optimizing resource allocation and ensuring the efficiency and reliability of next generation O-ISAC systems.

• Real-time practice: At present, the existing O-ISAC systems are critically limited to a small-scale level. For instance, the existing systems are only validated through theoretical and simulation analysis. Thus, there lies a notable research gap to validate the feasibility of this technology in real-time practical scenarios. The existing contributions have been made at the academic research level and only a few prototypes have been developed. However, the validation of academic research through industrial deployment and commercialization plays a pivotal role to advance any technology. Thus, there is a need for experimental field trials and the development of advanced practical O-ISAC systems to deploy in real-time environments such as traffic monitoring, toxic gas monitoring, bridge diagnostics, high-speed train monitoring, seismic analyses, healthcare centers, and smart cities. • Other salient features: As a promising complement to RF-ISAC, widespread research contributions are urgently required for future O-ISAC systems. In the case of waveform design for O-ISAC, optical OFDM schemes such as DCO-OFDM, ACO-OFDM etc., must be investigated to offer additional DoF for joint optimization. Additionally, novel performance metrics must be introduced for different sensing operations such as recognition, tracking, and detection to enhance the sensing performance of O-ISAC and save the scarce power and energy resources. Furthermore, angular domain sensing also remains an unexplored research direction in O-ISAC, which requires cost-effective and robust beamforming designs for optical sensors. With the emergence of photonic technologies like optical phased arrays and MEMS, O-ISAC systems can precisely sense and design a map of the surrounding environment based on the angular range profile, which paves a solid foundation to perform multiobjective sensing tasks in future 6G IoE networks. • Hardware cost and deployment: Considering the wide availability and hardware cost of optical transmitters like LEDs, some works have reported LED-aided O-ISAC system designs [6]. While these LED-assisted systems efficiently integrate optical communication and optical sensing, their incorporation is presently restricted by the concurrent use of transmitted signals. However, O-ISAC systems extend beyond simple signal sharing and hardware. Such O-ISAC systems are inherently complementary: optical communication offers required illumination to perform optical sensing, while optical sensing provides environmental information that can substantially improve optical communication. This dual functionality does not only enhance resource allocation but also opens up new directions for novel applications in diverse scenarios. Several new works have developed O-ISAC frameworks based on cost-efficient LEDs [7]. The authors in Ref. [6] briefly discussed the impact of considering IM/DD scheme for optical communication and pinhole imaging principle for optical sensing which provide a compelling advantage over RF-ISAC. For instance, the key benefit of using pinhole imaging sensing principle lied in its potential to project reflected optical signals from both the target objects and non-target objects onto distinct spatial coordinates. This spatial isolation of echo signals can significantly mitigate interference from non-target objects, thereby mitigating massive echo challenges. Future works should focus on exploring conventional boundaries to pave the way for more resource-efficient, cost-efficient, and intelligent O-ISAC systems. • Cross-domain implementation: In diverse application scenarios of O-ISAC, cross-domain data transmission is also inevitably essential. For example, optical transmission from air to underwater environments must keep account of not only the fundamental differences between atmospheric and underwater channels, but also the impacts of optical refraction and water surface waves on transmission. In a recent work, the authors proposed a channel model for blue-green vortex beams, accounting for the impacts of oceanic turbulence,

the air-sea interface, and atmospheric turbulence [168]. Meanwhile, recent developments into air-sea cross-boundary communications have focused on analyzing critical challenges and explored possible research aspects to achieve integrated space air-ground-ocean networks towards 6G [169]. In O-ISAC scenarios, multiple wavelength bands can be employed simultaneously for sensing and communication; therefore, it is essential to design a unified framework or channel model to accommodate multi-band optical transmissions with varying wavelengths. These channel models should also consider the realistic condition of atmospheric turbulence, beam alignment sensitivity, and pointing errors. The key objective of designing an optical channel model for O-ISAC is to comprehensively analyze the attenuation caused by optical characteristics like scattering, refraction, and reflection, as well as stochastic channel characteristics resulting from terrain/environmental factors like shadowing, turbulence, and multipath propagation. Furthermore, such channel models can integrate wavelength and medium-dependent factors to introduce intelligent wireless channel model with diverse applicability. • Collaborative integration: Even though research activities on OISAC are rapidly progressing, it still remains in an infancy level. The existing works on O-ISAC primarily emphasize on specialized optical system configurations, considering communication and sensing as separate tasks and providing ensuring limited evidence of their collaborative framework. Future O-ISAC systems will go beyond simply co-locating sensing and communication functions within a single device, harnessing their integration to maximize efficiency and strengthen application capabilities. Similarly, the lack of a comprehensive framework restricts its further developments, and

critical aspects like design methodologies, optimization strategies, performance metrics, theoretical models, hardware designs remain unexplored. In addition, the absence of real-time implementation, practical validations, and specific application scenarios critically limit the deployment of corresponding technologies and theories. Consequently, it is crucially important to explore the intrinsic interaction between optical sensing and optical communication, addressing theoretical limits, key technologies, and emerging use cases, in order to fully characterize the design and development of robust O-ISAC research framework. In a recent study, the authors provided a concrete discussion about O-ISAC, encompassing the system design, fundamental theory, and application scenarios, with the aim to jointly address communication and sensing requirements within a unified optimization framework [24]. • Data fusion with learning algorithms: Data fusion in integration with advanced learning tools can further revolutionize the next generation O-ISAC systems. By combining diverse information sources such as contextual data, communication feedback, sensor readings, O-ISAC will become more adaptable, resilient, and robust. To this end, ML, particularly RL and DL [170], can effectively extract complex patterns from data fusion, thereby leading to more precise predictions, estimations, resource management, and adaptive decision-making strategies [164]. Similarly, FL can comprehensively tackle privacy and security concerns by integrating data received from distributed nodes. In this context, multi-model learning such as multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) can be used to leverage heterogeneous sensing and communication data for enhanced environmental monitoring and situational awareness. Some existing issues such as energy efficiency, low-latency, and model robustness in harsh or dynamic scenarios (underground, underwater) may need innovative O-ISAC solutions in terms of signal processing, channel prediction and estimation, and optimal resource allocation. Collaborative research focusing on innovative designs and hybrid mechanism will empower seamless O-ISAC implementation in diverse applications.
